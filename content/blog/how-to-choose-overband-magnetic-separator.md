# How to Choose an Overband Magnetic Separator for Conveyor Belt Systems

## Introduction

Choosing an overband magnetic separator is not only a matter of selecting a model from a catalog. In real conveyor applications, the correct separator depends on the material being conveyed, conveyor belt width, belt speed, burden depth, iron size, installation position and the amount of metal contamination in the line.

For overseas buyers, this decision is often made before a purchase order, a plant upgrade, a recycling line installation or an equipment replacement. A buyer may ask a simple question such as "What size overband magnet do I need for a 1000 mm belt?" but the correct answer requires several working-condition details.

This guide explains how to choose an overband magnetic separator for bulk material handling systems. It is written for importers, distributors, recycling plants, mining companies, quarry operators, cement plants, coal handling facilities, EPC contractors and OEM equipment manufacturers who need a practical selection framework before requesting a quotation.

## What Is an Overband Magnetic Separator?

An overband magnetic separator is a magnetic separation device installed above a conveyor belt to remove ferrous metals from conveyed material. It is commonly used to capture tramp iron, protect downstream equipment and improve metal recovery.

The separator usually includes a magnetic block, frame, discharge belt, motor and support structure. When ferrous metal passes under the magnet, it is attracted upward and carried away by the self-cleaning belt. The discharged metal falls to the side of the conveyor or into a collection area.

This design is especially useful for continuous production lines where manual cleaning would create downtime or require frequent labor.

## Common Applications

Overband magnetic separators are widely used in:

- Waste recycling and construction waste processing
- Mining and ore handling
- Quarrying, sand and aggregate production
- Cement plants and raw material conveying
- Coal handling and power plants
- Wood processing and biomass lines
- Ports, terminals and bulk material transfer systems

The main goal may vary. In a recycling plant, the separator may be used to recover ferrous metals. In a mining or quarry line, the main purpose may be crusher protection. In a cement or coal handling line, the buyer may want to reduce equipment damage and avoid unplanned downtime.

## Step 1: Confirm the Conveyor Belt Width

Conveyor belt width is the first parameter most suppliers will ask for. Common belt widths include 500 mm, 600 mm, 800 mm, 1000 mm, 1200 mm, 1400 mm and larger widths depending on the project.

The magnetic separator should cover the effective material flow area, not only the nominal belt width. If the material stream is centered and controlled, a standard model may be suitable. If the material is unevenly distributed or the conveyor has a wide loading area, the supplier may recommend a larger magnetic surface or a different installation position.

For example, a separator for an 800 mm belt may not be suitable for a 1200 mm belt even if the tramp iron size looks similar. Wider belts normally require a wider magnetic field and a stronger structural design.

## Step 2: Check the Material Layer Depth

Material layer depth, also called burden depth, is critical because the magnet must attract ferrous metal through the material layer. If the burden is shallow, separation is easier. If the material layer is thick, wet, compacted or irregular, a stronger magnetic field or a lower installation height may be required.

When preparing an inquiry, buyers should provide:

- Average material layer thickness
- Maximum burden depth
- Material density or approximate bulk weight
- Moisture level
- Particle size range
- Whether the material is free-flowing or sticky

For example, separating nails or small iron pieces from wood chips is different from removing heavy tramp iron from a thick ore burden. The magnet selection must reflect the actual burden depth, not just the belt width.

## Step 3: Identify the Tramp Iron Size and Amount

The size, shape and frequency of ferrous contamination directly affect separator selection.

Small ferrous particles may require a different magnetic configuration than large bolts, teeth, rods or plates. Large heavy metal pieces may need stronger attraction and better discharge clearance. If the iron content is frequent, a self-cleaning overband separator is usually more suitable than a manual-cleaning suspended magnet.

Useful information includes:

- Typical iron size
- Maximum iron size
- Estimated iron quantity per hour or per shift
- Whether the iron is mixed deep in the burden or visible on the surface
- Whether the goal is equipment protection or metal recovery

In project communication, photos or short videos of the contaminated material can be very helpful. They allow the supplier to understand the real working condition instead of relying only on written descriptions.

## Step 4: Choose Manual Cleaning or Self-Cleaning

There are two common cleaning methods.

### Manual-Cleaning Suspended Magnet

A manual-cleaning suspended magnet is suitable when iron contamination is occasional and the material line can tolerate periodic cleaning. The magnet captures ferrous metal on its bottom surface, and workers remove the metal manually using non-magnetic tools or gloves.

This option is simpler and often lower in initial cost. However, it is not ideal when the line has frequent metal contamination or continuous production requirements.

### Self-Cleaning Overband Magnetic Separator

A self-cleaning overband separator uses a moving discharge belt to automatically carry captured ferrous metal away from the magnetic field. This design is better for continuous operation, higher iron content and applications where manual cleaning would interrupt production.

For recycling, mining, quarrying and cement plants, self-cleaning models are often preferred because they reduce labor and improve operational consistency.

## Step 5: Decide the Installation Position

Overband magnetic separators can be installed in different positions depending on conveyor layout.

### Cross-Belt Installation

In cross-belt installation, the separator is mounted across the conveyor. Captured iron is discharged to the side. This arrangement is common when side discharge space is available.

### In-Line Installation

In in-line installation, the separator is mounted over the head pulley area, with the material leaving the conveyor. This can improve separation efficiency in some cases because the material is loosening as it leaves the belt.

The best installation position depends on conveyor structure, available space, discharge direction, maintenance access and the point where tramp iron should be removed before downstream equipment.

## Step 6: Confirm Suspension Height

Suspension height is the distance between the magnet surface and the material surface or conveyor belt. A higher suspension height requires a stronger magnetic field. If the magnet is installed too high, it may not capture the target iron reliably.

Before quotation, buyers should provide:

- Distance from conveyor belt to available installation frame
- Material layer depth
- Desired clearance for large lumps
- Whether the separator is installed before a crusher, screen or transfer point

In many cases, installation drawings or site photos help prevent model mismatch. A good supplier will not only quote a standard model but also ask how the separator will be mounted.

## Step 7: Consider Belt Speed and Material Flow

Conveyor belt speed affects how long ferrous metal remains under the magnetic field. Faster belt speeds reduce the available attraction time. Heavy material flow may also bury tramp iron under the burden.

If the belt speed is high or the burden is heavy, the separator may need stronger magnetic force, better positioning or a different installation method.

Typical RFQ information should include:

- Belt speed
- Capacity per hour
- Material loading condition
- Conveyor inclination
- Transfer point layout

## Step 8: Match the Separator to the Industry

Different industries have different selection priorities.

### Waste Recycling

Recycling lines often need self-cleaning separators because ferrous metal appears frequently. The goal may include both metal recovery and downstream equipment protection.

### Mining and Quarrying

Mining and quarry lines often focus on crusher protection. The separator must handle heavy burden, dust, vibration and large tramp iron.

### Cement and Coal Handling

Cement and coal lines need stable operation, reliable structure and good protection against equipment damage. Dust resistance and bearing protection should be considered.

### Food, Grain and Light Materials

For lighter materials, different magnetic components such as magnetic grids, bars or drums may be more suitable than a heavy overband separator, depending on the process.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Choosing Only by Belt Width

Belt width is important, but it is not enough. Two conveyors with the same belt width may require different separators if one has a shallow layer of wood chips and the other has a thick layer of ore.

### Ignoring Suspension Height

If the magnet is installed too high, separation performance may drop. Always confirm installation space before ordering.

### Using Manual Cleaning on a Continuous Line

Manual-cleaning magnets can be suitable for occasional tramp iron, but they are not ideal for lines with frequent metal contamination.

### Not Planning Discharge Space

Self-cleaning separators need enough space for discharged iron. The discharge direction and collection area should be planned before installation.

## FAQ

### What information is needed to choose an overband magnetic separator?

You should provide material type, conveyor belt width, belt speed, material layer depth, suspension height, iron size, iron amount and installation position. Photos or drawings are also helpful.

### Is a permanent overband separator better than an electromagnetic separator?

It depends on the application. Permanent overband separators require no power for the magnetic field and are suitable for many continuous tramp iron removal applications. Electromagnetic separators may be preferred for deeper burden, adjustable magnetic intensity or heavy-duty conditions.

### Can an overband magnetic separator be customized?

Yes. Common customization includes magnetic strength, equipment size, belt width, installation structure, discharge direction, color, logo and packaging.

### Where should the separator be installed?

It can be installed across the belt or in-line over the head pulley. The best position depends on conveyor layout, material flow and where tramp iron must be removed.

### Why is self-cleaning important?

Self-cleaning reduces manual labor and supports continuous operation. It is usually recommended when ferrous contamination appears frequently.

## Conclusion

Choosing an overband magnetic separator requires more than selecting a catalog model. The correct choice depends on conveyor width, material burden, tramp iron size, cleaning method, installation height, working environment and application goal.

For the best result, buyers should prepare real working-condition data before requesting a quotation. This helps the supplier recommend a suitable model, avoid undersizing and reduce installation problems.
